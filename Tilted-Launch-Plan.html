♠  Executive Summary

The bet, and how we'll test it

Tilted is "Words With Friends, but poker." An asynchronous heads-up Hold'em game where two friends play ten hands in parallel off one shared chip stack. The shared-stack mechanic is genuinely novel — every bet in one hand drains what you can commit to the other nine — and the async turn model serves a real, underserved persona: the poker-loving professional with five free minutes, not a free hour. No real money, ever. The product is the ritual between two friends, not a casino.

The game is validated as fun at n=2: TJ and Stephen have played voluntarily for weeks and iterated the rules into something both enjoy. Everything beyond that is a hypothesis. This plan treats the launch the way The Lean Startup treats everything: as a sequence of experiments designed to convert leap-of-faith assumptions into validated learning as cheaply and quickly as possible — with explicit pivot-or-persevere gates so we never coast on vanity metrics.

The two leap-of-faith assumptions

Value hypothesis: Pairs of friends who like poker will keep playing Tilted voluntarily, week after week, as a standing ritual — without TJ personally nudging them. Measured by pair retention, not downloads.

Growth hypothesis: People who love Tilted will pull their friends into it — the invite is the product's front door, because you literally cannot play alone. Measured by invites sent per retained player and invite → active-pair conversion.

The plan in one paragraph

Phase 0 hardens the current build into a Minimum Lovable Product that survives pairs who aren't TJ+Stephen (turn deadlines, onboarding, invite flow, instrumentation). Phase 1 seeds 10–20 pairs from TJ's personal network — not as a marketing push but as a learning lab, with weekly interviews and a pivot-on-the-MLP loop. Phase 2 takes the game public as a portfolio of channel experiments (Hacker News, Product Hunt, poker communities, short-form video, ASO), each with a written hypothesis, a budget, and a kill criterion. Phase 3 turns on revenue (freemium subscription, tested with a fake-door experiment before any code is written) and doubles down on whichever growth loop the Phase 2 data validates — with a pivot catalog ready if the data says the pair model isn't the business.

What success looks like at each gate

PhaseQuestion it answersGate to advance

Honest framing: the realistic outcome distribution for any consumer game is harsh. This plan is designed so that every phase produces either a growing business or cheap, fast, decisive learning — and TJ is job-hunting in parallel, so gates double as "how much more of my time does this deserve?" checkpoints.

The one metric that matters

Active Pairs — pairs with ≥ 1 completed round in the trailing 7 days.

Everything in this plan exists to move Active Pairs and its quality shadow, Pair W4 Retention. Downloads, impressions, waitlist signups, and press are inputs, never outcomes.

Why pairs, not users? A solo Tilted install is inventory, not a customer. The unit of value, retention, and revenue is the pair. Every metric in this plan is cohorted by pair-formation week.

The Lean Startup loop, applied to Tilted

Every initiative in this plan is written as an experiment: hypothesis → smallest possible test → actionable metric → green/red criteria. Three disciplines from the book that we adopt as house rules:

  • Validated learning over output. A week that ships nothing but kills a bad assumption is a good week. A week that ships a feature nobody's behavior changes for is waste ("achieving failure").
  • Innovation accounting. For each phase: (1) establish a real baseline with an MVP-grade test, (2) tune the engine toward the ideal with small batches, (3) hold a scheduled pivot-or-persevere meeting — on the calendar, every 6 weeks, with Stephen or a trusted outsider present to keep it honest.
  • Actionable, not vanity. Every reported number must be cohort-based, per-pair, and tied to a decision. "12,000 downloads" is banned vocabulary; "the March cohort's pair-activation rate rose from 31% → 44% after the new onboarding" is the house style.

Engine of growth: sticky first, viral second

Lean Startup names three engines — sticky (retention), viral (referral), paid (LTV > CAC). Tilted's natural engine is sticky (a standing ritual between friends) with a structurally forced viral component (you must invite someone to play at all). Paid acquisition is out of scope until a subscription proves LTV — spending money to acquire users for a free app with unproven retention is the classic way to burn savings on vanity metrics. The plan's ordering follows: prove sticky (Phases 0–1), prove viral (Phase 2), then consider paid (late Phase 3, only if unit economics exist).

Kanban rule (small batches): at most one product experiment and one distribution experiment in flight at a time. Solo-founder WIP limits are the difference between a learning loop and a pile of half-finished bets.

Phase 0 exit test (run it literally)

Hand the TestFlight link to two friends who have never seen the game, in different cities, and say nothing but "try this." They form a pair, complete three rounds, and neither texts TJ a question. Until this passes, no marketing dollar or hour is spent.

Do-not-do list (scope discipline): no matchmaking, no chat, no Android, no web client, no cosmetics, no tournament mode, no rewrite. Each is a Phase 2/3 option to be earned by data, not a Phase 0 to-do.

♦  Phase 1 · Weeks 5–10

Friends beta: a learning lab, not a launch

Phase 1's product is validated learning about the value hypothesis, drawn from TJ's personal network — engineer friends, ex-colleagues, poker buddies — rather than the Marin tournament crowd (older, lighter app usage, and money-poker players are the wrong first segment for a no-money game; they remain a Phase 2 experiment, not the seed). Target: 10–20 pairs (~20–40 players), recruited personally, one at a time.

Recruiting mechanics

  • Recruit pairs, not people. The ask is "you and a friend," never "you." A solo recruit who has to find their own partner is a coin-flip; a recruited pair is a live experiment. Where possible TJ plays match #1 with each recruit personally (concierge onboarding), then pushes them to start a match with their own friend — that second match is the one that counts.
  • Segment deliberately. Aim for a spread: poker-serious vs. casual; close friends vs. coworkers; same-city vs. long-distance. The goal is to learn which kind of pair Tilted retains — that's the Phase 2 targeting input.
  • Set the contract. "It's a beta, it's free, I'll ask you questions weekly, and I'll change things under your feet." Beta users who know they're co-developing forgive rough edges and give better feedback.

The measurement plan

Value signal
Pair W4 retention
% of pairs completing ≥1 round in week 4 after forming. Benchmark to beat: ≥ 50% at this stage (friends inflate it; strangers will halve it).
Love signal
Unprompted rematch
A pair finishes a match (someone busts) and starts a new one without any nudge. The single strongest signal the ritual is real.
Growth foreshadow
2nd-degree invites
Beta users inviting people TJ doesn't know. Even 3–5 occurrences de-risks the whole Phase 2 viral thesis.

Qualitative engine (this is where the MLP pivots happen)

  • Weekly 20-minute calls with 3–4 rotating players. Script: What's the moment you open the app? What did you almost quit over? Retell your favorite hand — can they, without looking? (the spec's own §19 love test). What would you show a friend?
  • The Sean Ellis question at week 3: "How disappointed would you be if you could no longer play Tilted?" ≥ 40% "very disappointed" among active players is the classic product-market-fit proxy — at this sample size treat it as directional, not gospel.
  • Rule experiments in small batches: hand count (10 vs. 5?), turn deadline length, blind escalation. One variable at a time, one cohort at a time — the spec was built for cheap rule changes; use that superpower now, before scale makes experiments slow.
  • Watch three first-runs live (screen-share or in person). Nothing finds onboarding failure faster than watching a friend squint at the cart screen.
Phase 1 pivot-or-persevere gate (week ~10)

Persevere to Phase 2 if: ≥ 50% pair W4 retention, ≥ 5 unprompted rematches, and at least a handful of players can retell a hand from memory.

Tune first if activation is weak but retained pairs love it (onboarding/liveness problem, not a fun problem) — fix, re-cohort, re-measure; 2–3 week loops.

Consider a pivot if even friends-of-TJ pairs quietly stop by week 3: the honest reading is that the ritual isn't self-sustaining yet. Go to the pivot catalog (p. 12) — likely candidates: hand-count/pace changes (zoom-in on what retained pairs loved) or group-play formats.

♠  Phase 2 · Weeks ~11–20

Public launch: a portfolio of channel experiments

Phase 2 answers the growth hypothesis: can strangers discover Tilted, pull a friend in, and stick? The Lean move is to treat "launch" not as an event but as 6–8 independent channel experiments, each with a hypothesis, a budget (mostly hours), and a kill criterion — run at a WIP limit of one at a time, weekly review. Two channels earning a green light matters more than one big spike.

First: pick the message (test before the launch, not during)

PositioningStrengthRisk
P1"Words With Friends, but poker."Instant comprehension; explains async + friends in four words; nostalgic warmth.Dated reference for under-30s; sets casual expectations for a strategic game.
P2"Poker for people with five minutes."Speaks straight to the persona's constraint; strong for busy-professional channels (HN, LinkedIn).Undersells the novel mechanic; sounds like every casual game's promise.
P3"Ten hands. One stack. A new kind of poker."Leads with the genuinely new thing; intrigues poker natives; premium, ownable.Requires a beat of explanation; abstraction risk for casual players.

How to choose: a $0 smoke test. One landing page (tilted.app), three headline variants rotated (or three cheap $100 ad sets if impatient), measure email-capture / TestFlight-tap rate per variant. Secondary read: which one Phase 1 players spontaneously repeat when describing the game to friends — the message that survives word-of-mouth wins. Working hypothesis: P3 as the brand line, P1 as the explainer sentence that follows it everywhere.

Sequencing: three options

Option A · RecommendedRolling thunder

One channel per week-or-two, in rising order of audience size and falling order of forgiveness: poker Reddit/Discords → Show HN → short-form video → Product Hunt. Each is a clean experiment with its own cohort; learnings from one improve the next; a flop in a small pond costs nothing. Fits solo capacity and the WIP-limit rule. Tradeoff: no big coordinated splash, less press-worthy.

Option BBig bang

Coordinate HN + Product Hunt + Reddit + press outreach in one week for compounding attention and a shot at "trending" flywheels. Tradeoff: one roll of the dice; confounded cohorts (you can't tell which channel worked); a server or App Store hiccup burns every channel at once. Only sensible if Phase 1 metrics are exceptional and the infra has been load-tested.

Option CContent-first slow burn

6–8 weeks of build-in-public posts and daily "interesting Tilted hands" content before any launch moment, building a waitlist; launch to the warm list. Tradeoff: slowest path to stranger-retention data — and retention data is the whole point of Phase 2. Best as a background layer under Option A (see channels E/F), not a standalone strategy.

♥  Phase 2 · Channel Experiment Portfolio (1 of 2)

Each channel is a falsifiable bet

Cost: ~2 days prep

A · Show HN (Hacker News)

Hypothesis
HN's audience is the persona (analytical professionals, five free minutes, love novel mechanics). An engineer-founder story about the shared-stack design + pure-core architecture earns front-page attention that converts to pairs.
Test
"Show HN: I built a poker variant where you play 10 hands at once off one stack." Post mid-week ~8–9am PT. Lead comment: design rationale, the portfolio-decision insight, honest state of the app. TJ answers comments all day.
Green
≥ 100 installs, ≥ 25 pairs formed, W1 pair retention within 20% of friends-cohort baseline.
Red / learn
Traffic but no pairs → invite funnel is the bottleneck (prioritize web challenge-link). No traffic → the story angle, not the game, failed; retry once in 6–8 wks with a new angle (e.g., open-sourcing the engine).
Cost: ~3–4 days prep

B · Product Hunt

Hypothesis
PH's early-adopter crowd converts on novelty and polish; a top-10 day yields a durable badge, backlinks/SEO, and a spike big enough to read stranger-retention statistically.
Test
Launch after HN learnings are folded in. Assets: 30-sec demo video of one full turn (board pass → cart → submit), 5 screenshots, maker story. Rally Phase 1 beta users for genuine day-of comments.
Green
≥ 300 installs and ≥ 50 pairs in launch week; measurable waitlist/press spillover.
Red / learn
Upvotes without installs → listing promises unclear (message problem). Installs without pairs → same funnel lesson as A, now confirmed on a second audience.

C · Poker communities (r/poker, poker Discords, 2+2 forums)

Hypothesis
Poker natives will engage with the strategy of the mechanic — bankroll allocation across 10 simultaneous spots is a genuinely new puzzle — even though (because!) no real money is involved.
Test
Don't advertise; post spots. "You have 2,000 chips across these 10 hands — how do you allocate?" screenshots with real decisions, participate honestly as the builder when asked. One post/week per community, respecting each community's self-promo norms (build karma first; some subs need mod pre-clearance).
Green
Threads sustain organic debate; ≥ 10 pairs/mo attributable (ask-at-signup "where did you hear?"); repeat commenters recognizable in-app.
Red / learn
"No real money = pointless" dominates → money-poker natives are the wrong segment; reweight toward casual/strategy-gamer channels (D, E) and note the segment learning for Phase 3 targeting.
Cost: ~1 day/wk for 6 wks

D · Short-form video (TikTok / Reels / YouTube Shorts)

Hypothesis
Poker content massively over-performs on short-form; the end-of-round reveal (queued all-ins running out one by one) is native vertical-video drama; the cart's "fund one jam by un-betting another hand" is a hook nobody has seen.
Test
Produce 15–20 clips before judging anything (algorithmic variance demands volume): reveal moments, "how would you play this?" spots, dev-log snippets. Screen-recorded, captioned, zero production value to start.
Green
Any clip > 50k views with install lift visible in that week's cohort; a repeatable format identified.
Red / learn
20 clips, all flat → park the channel; revisit post-Phase-3 with creator partnerships instead of first-party content.

Persevere

Stranger-cohort pair W4 retention ≥ 25%, at least one channel produces pairs repeatably (two consecutive green months), and referral k ≥ 0.3.

→ Proceed to Phase 3: monetize and double down on the winning channel. This is a real consumer game.

Tune

Retained pairs love it but a single funnel stage leaks badly (e.g., invite → partner-install < 25%, or activation < 50%).

→ Not a pivot — an engineering sprint aimed at the leak (web challenge-link for pairing; onboarding rework for activation; deadlines/nudges for W1). Re-cohort, re-measure, 4-week loops. Growth-hypothesis problems have product solutions.

Stranger pairs activate but don't retain (< 15% W4) across two tuned cohorts — strangers-to-TJ confirm the fun, but not the habit.

  • Never average friends-cohorts with stranger-cohorts — Phase 1 friends will flatter every number.
  • If a week's report can't name the decision a number informs, the number doesn't go in the report.

♦  Phase 3 · Revenue

Monetization: test demand before writing code

Model: freemium subscription. Free tier = the complete core game, one active match per pair — the invite loop must never hit a paywall. Paid tier = power features for the hooked. Poker players' willingness to pay recurring money for tools is proven at the high end (solver subscriptions run $39–149/mo); Tilted prices as a consumer game, not a pro tool.

Step 1 — Fake-door test (during Phase 2, zero build cost)

Ship a "Tilted Plus" screen in Settings listing candidate features and a price, with a single button: "Join the waitlist." No purchase, no entitlement code — just measure who taps, at which displayed price (rotate $1.99 / $3.99 / $5.99 monthly across cohorts), against how active they are. This is the Lean answer to "what would you pay?" — measure behavior, never ask opinions. A 60-second survey after the tap (Van Westendorp's four price questions) adds a second, cheaper signal.

Decision rule: ≥ 5% of W4-retained players tap at any price point → build the subscription. Below that, revenue waits and Phase 3 becomes growth-only until retention deepens.

Step 2 — Choose the gates (options, with pros/cons)

Gate candidateProsCons / risks
Concurrent matches (free: 1 active match; Plus: unlimited)Cleanest gate: only power users hit it; monetizes exactly the most-retained; server already supports per-pair concurrency.Caps the social graph's growth if set too tight — consider free = 2 before free = 1.

Working recommendation: launch Plus = concurrent matches + templates; add the stats pack as the retention-deepener in the following quarter. Never gate history.

Step 3 — Price & package experiments

  • Price points to test: $2.99/mo · $19.99/yr (the anchor; push annual hard — churn insurance for a ritual product) · $29.99 "Founding Member" lifetime, first 500 only: early cash, commitment signal, and a thank-you to beta pairs. Cohort A/B via StoreKit; settle within two cohorts.
  • Pair pricing (differentiator): a "Table" plan (~$29.99/yr) covering both players in a pair. Nobody in the category does this; it matches the unit of value and turns upgrading into a gift. Test against solo pricing.
  • Web payments later: if the web challenge-link client ships (p. 11), web-side subscription via Stripe skips Apple's 15–30% — a margin option, not a launch requirement (mind Apple's evolving external-purchase rules at the time).
  • Revenue reality check (write it down, stay sane): 1,000 retained players × 5% paid × ~$20/yr ≈ $1k/yr; 20,000 × 8% ≈ $32k/yr. Subscription revenue follows retention scale — which is why Phases 1–2 gate Phase 3, and why the pivot catalog includes bigger-swing options.

Sequencing principle

Each build above is 2–6 weeks of solo work. Choose by measured constraint, not excitement — the Lean discipline that matters most when the builder is also the founder and the marketer. Ship → re-cohort → re-measure → next pivot-or-persevere meeting. Six-week cycles, forever.

On paid acquisition (the third engine): becomes discussable only when subscription data yields an LTV, and only into the proven channel's lookalike audience. Rule of thumb: don't spend a dollar on ads until blended payback < 6 months is plausible on paper — for a $20/yr product that means CAC under ~$10, which is achievable only with a strong viral assist (k ≥ 0.5) doing half the work.

♠  Phase 3 · The Pivot Catalog

Pre-committed pivots (decided calmly, in advance)

Lean Startup's sharpest observation: founders pivot too late because the decision is made under sunk-cost pressure. So the pivot options are written now, each with the evidence that would trigger it. If a trigger fires at a scheduled pivot-or-persevere meeting, the corresponding pivot gets a 6-week validation cycle of its own — a pivot is a new strategy with a new baseline, not an admission of failure. The core asset in every scenario: a validated-fun poker mechanic, a rules-flexible engine, and everything learned about who plays and why.

Pivot (Lean type)What Tilted becomesTrigger evidence
Leagues-first (customer-segment) The group product: home games, friend groups, and company channels running month-long round-robin seasons; pairs become the atom inside a group ritual. Pairs churn but clustered adoption keeps appearing (channel H, second-degree invites arriving in groups); interviews say "we'd play if the whole crew was in."
Open-Seat-first (engine-of-growth: sticky→viral-lite) Solo-friendly: download → matched with a rival in 60 seconds → rivalry record does the retention work. Friends remain a feature, not the funnel. Acquisition is easy but pairing is the immovable leak even after the web client; solo installs keep asking for "anyone to play with."
Format zoom-in (zoom-in) The one thing everyone loved, sharpened: e.g., 5-hand quick rounds; or a daily async "allocation puzzle" (same 10 dealt hands for everyone, global leaderboard) as the front door to matches. Interviews and behavior isolate one loved element (the cart decision, the reveal) while full matches feel long; completion drops mid-match but round-one engagement is universal.
Platform pivot (channel) Web-first (or iMessage-app-first): the game lives where invites already travel; native apps become the premium shell. Web challenge-link players engage at ≥ 70% of app players and the install step is measurably the growth ceiling.
Study-tool pivot (customer-need) A poker-training product: the parallel-hands format as drill engine — range quizzes, allocation puzzles, spaced repetition. Sells to the money-poker crowd (channel C's skeptics) at tool prices, $10–20/mo. Retention as a game fails, but poker-serious users keep calling the mechanic "great practice"; C's threads engage with spots while ignoring the app.
Shelve gracefully (the honest option) Open-source the engine, write the retrospective (genuinely valuable content + job-market signal), keep the server alive cheaply for the pairs who love it. TJ exits with reputation, a portfolio piece, and the option to return. Two full pivot cycles fail their gates; Active Pairs plateaus < 100 despite green channels; or the job hunt converts and hours drop below viability. Deciding this coolly in advance is what makes every earlier bet safe to take.
The standing rule

Every pivot-or-persevere meeting (weeks ~10, ~20, then every 6 weeks) ends with a one-page memo: baseline numbers, what was learned, the verdict, and the next cycle's single hypothesis. Ten minutes of writing; it is the difference between iterating and wandering — and it's excellent build-in-public content besides.
